Ancient Rome expanded its defenses across several major phases, evolving from early ritual boundaries and localized hill fortifications into massive brick-faced concrete circuits.

1. The Pomerium and Primitive Walls

• Pomerium: The sacred, religious boundary of Rome, originally established by Romulus. It separated the civic and political interior (ager urbanus) from foreign territory.
• Romulus's Square Palatine Wall (c. 753 BCE): A primitive, enclosed circuit protecting the Palatine Hill with early portals like the Porta Mugonia, Porta Romulana, and Carmenta Gate.

2. The Servian Wall (4th Century BCE)

• Overview: The first truly monumental defensive circuit of Rome, built after the sacking of the city by the Gauls around 390 BCE.
• Dimensions: Roughly 7 to 11 miles (about 11–12 km) long, standing around 35 feet (10.6 m) high and 12 feet thick, enclosing roughly 1,000 acres across the seven hills of Rome.
• Materials: Built initially of local cappellaccio tuff, then heavily reinforced and rebuilt using higher-quality Grotta Oscura tuff blocks fitted tightly together.
• Key Gates: Featured around 15 main gates, including Porta Capena, Porta Collina, Porta Esquilina, and Porta Carmentalis.

3. The Aurelian Wall (271–275 CE)

• Overview: Built under Emperor Aurelian (and completed by Probus) amid a severe crisis of the Roman Empire to protect a vastly expanded urban population.
• Dimensions: Spanned roughly 12 to 19 miles (19–20 km) long, enclosing about 3,000 acres (1,400 hectares). Originally stood around 24 feet (7.2 m) high, later raised to 35 feet (10.6 m) under General Stilicho.
• Materials & Architecture: Constructed using durable brick-faced concrete (opus caementicium). It incorporated existing structures (such as aqueduct arches and tombs like the Tomb of the Baker and the Arch of Claudius at Porta Maggiore) into the curtain.
• Towers and Defense: Reinforced with 381 to 383 square or rectangular defensive towers spaced roughly every 100 feet (30 m) to accommodate artillery like ballistae, alongside thousands of arrow slots.
• Major Gates: Featured 18 main portals strategically aligned with major consular roads:
	• Porta San Sebastiano (Porta Appia): One of the largest and best-preserved southern gates, housing the Museo delle Mura.
	• Porta Maggiore (Praenestina/Labicana): A monumental double-arched gate formed from an old Claudian aqueduct.
	• Porta San Paolo (Ostiensis): The gateway leading toward Ostia.
	• Porta Flaminia (Porta del Popolo): The northern gate opening to the Via Flaminia.
	• Other notable gates: Porta Salaria, Porta Pinciana, Porta Tiburtina, Porta Asinaria, Porta Latina, and Porta San Pancrazio.
